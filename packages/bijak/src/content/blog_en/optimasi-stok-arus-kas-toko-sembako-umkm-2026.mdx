### Quick Audit (60 Seconds)
* **The Core Issue**: Capital often rots in slow-moving or expired inventory, locking up to 40% of daily assets.
* **The Fix**: Strict FIFO/FEFO discipline, tracking turnover ratios, and migrating to self-hosted digital logs.
* **The Result**: 35% boost in capital efficiency and an 85% reduction in inventory loss.

Dusty boxes of cooking oil and instant noodles in your warehouse aren't just clutter—they represent stagnant cash. When inventory sits still while supplier invoices pile up, your business is 'asset-rich but cash-poor.' This is a common trap for grocery retailers who chase sales volume while ignoring the 'bleeding' caused by dead stock.

Evidence shows that increasing inventory turnover by 2.5x can free up 35% of your liquidity within 90 days. In a market where margins are razor-thin, cash flow isn't just a metric; it's your primary survival tool.

## Why Capital Velocity Matters

Grocery retail operates on thin margins, typically 2% to 8%. To make a real profit, you need speed. Your money must cycle from cash to inventory and back to cash as quickly as possible.

Stagnant stock causes three main problems: it locks up working capital, increases the risk of expiration, and wastes valuable shelf space. Healthy management is about balancing what's on the shelf with what's in the bank.

## Categorizing Your Inventory: Fast vs. Slow

Start by auditing your products based on how fast they move:

1. **Fast-Moving**: Essentials like rice, oil, and eggs (1-7 day cycle).
2. **Medium-Moving**: Detergents, soaps, and popular snacks (8-21 day cycle).
3. **Slow-Moving**: Specialty spices or hardware items (30+ day cycle).

To stop losing money on expired goods, enforce **FIFO (First-In, First-Out)** and **FEFO (First-Expired, First-Out)**. Always place the newest stock at the back and the oldest at the front.

## FAQ

**1. How often should I audit stock?**
Weekly for fast-moving staples; monthly for everything else.

**2. How do I clear slow-moving stock?**
Use bundling (e.g., pair a slow item with a best-seller at a discount) or run a clearance sale.

**3. When is it time for an ERP?**
When you hit 150+ transactions daily or manage more than one warehouse location.
