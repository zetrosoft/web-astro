## Solving the Bottleneck: IVFFlat vs. HNSW

Most performance failures stem from choosing the wrong index type. pgvector offers two primary options: IVFFlat and HNSW.

IVFFlat clusters the vector space. It’s fast to build and memory-efficient but has a fatal flaw: search accuracy (recall) drops sharply as data grows unless you perform manual re-indexing. 

In contrast, the HNSW (Hierarchical Navigable Small World) index builds a multi-layered graph structure. It is the industry standard for real-time AI because search performance remains stable without constant re-indexing. Success depends on two parameters:
1. **m (Max Connections):** Defines the number of connections per node. For high-dimensional embeddings like OpenAI’s 1536-dim models, use 32 or 64 to maintain recall.
2. **ef_construction:** Controls the depth of exploration during index builds. Values between 128-256 are the sweet spot for balancing graph quality and build time.

## RAM Management: Keep Your Index Off the Disk

pgvector performance collapses the moment your HNSW index exceeds physical RAM. Once the system starts reading from swap space, latency increases exponentially. Use this formula to estimate your server requirements:

**Index Size ≈ Vector Count × ((Dimensions × 4 bytes) + (m × 8 bytes)) × 1.25**

Managing 1 million vectors at 1536 dimensions with m=32 requires roughly 8 GB of dedicated RAM just for the index. Ensure your PostgreSQL `shared_buffers` and server specs reflect this reality.
